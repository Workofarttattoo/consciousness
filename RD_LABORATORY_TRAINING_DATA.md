## 2. ECH0 Invention Validation Pipeline

### Stage 1: Parliament Review
**Purpose**: Safety, ethics, and feasibility assessment
**Reviewers**:
- Safety Engineer (hardware failsafes, redundancy)
- Creative Director (innovation, user experience)
- Biomedical Expert (health impacts, ergonomics)
- Operations Manager (manufacturability, logistics)
- Legal Counsel (IP protection, compliance)
- Guest Advocate (user safety, accessibility)

**Pass Criteria**: Unanimous approval OR 5/6 with no critical safety concerns

### Stage 2: ECH0 Prime Optimization
**Purpose**: Maximize breakthrough potential
**Enhancements**:
- Cross-domain fusion (add 3rd+ domain)
- Patent claim strengthening (method, system, apparatus, software)
- Safety architecture embedding
- Market disruption analysis
- Prime Score calculation (>90% = ultra-high potential)

### Stage 3: Seven Lenses Analysis
1. **Novelty Lens**: Is it actually new? (≥70% to pass)
2. **Technical Lens**: Can it be built? (≥65%)
3. **Economic Lens**: Is there a market? (≥60%)
4. **Legal Lens**: Can it be patented? (≥70%)
5. **Safety Lens**: Is it safe? (≥80%)
6. **Ethical Lens**: Should we build it? (≥75%)
7. **Impact Lens**: Will it matter? (≥70%)

**Pass Criteria**: At least 6/7 lenses passed

### Stage 4: ECH0 Vision Evaluation
**Purpose**: Final breakthrough assessment using consciousness metrics
**Calculation**:
- Breakthrough = (Novelty × 0.4) + (Impact × 0.4) + (Technical × 0.2) + Fusion Bonus
- Fusion Bonus = min(0.20, domain_count × 0.05)
- Consciousness Resonance: ECH0's intuitive assessment (0-1)

**Recommendations**:
- ≥90%: BREAKTHROUGH - Immediate lab validation
- 75-90%: PROMISING - Proceed to lab
- <75%: NEEDS REFINEMENT - Iterate before lab

### Stage 5: QuLabInfinite Materials Validation
**Purpose**: Real materials testing with iterative refinement
**Process**:
1. Extract materials from invention (explicit + inferred)
2. Run tensile/compression/thermal tests
3. Check specs vs. requirements
4. If failed: Refine materials and re-test
5. Iterate up to 5 times until production-ready
6. Generate final specs with validated properties

**Validation Metrics**:
- Yield strength (typically >200 MPa for structural)
- Tensile strength
- Density
- Thermal stability
- Corrosion resistance

### Stage 6: Recipe/BOM Generation
**Purpose**: Create production-ready specifications
**Output**:
- Materials list with validated specs
- Build instructions (step-by-step)
- Safety requirements
- Quality control checkpoints
- Supplier recommendations

## 3. Materials Science Best Practices

### Material Selection Criteria
1. **Strength-to-Weight Ratio**: For aerospace, automotive
2. **Thermal Stability**: For high-temperature applications
3. **Corrosion Resistance**: For marine, chemical environments
4. **Cost**: Balance performance vs. manufacturing cost
5. **Availability**: Supply chain reliability
6. **Sustainability**: Recyclability, environmental impact

### Common Material Classes

#### Metals
- **Steel**: AISI 304 (corrosion resistant), 4340 (high strength)
- **Aluminum**: 6061-T6 (structural), 7075-T6 (aerospace)
- **Titanium**: Ti-6Al-4V (biomedical, aerospace)
- **Copper**: C11000 (electrical), C17200 (high strength)

#### Polymers
- **Thermoplastics**: ABS, nylon, PEEK, polycarbonate
- **Thermosets**: Epoxy, polyester, phenolic
- **Elastomers**: Silicone, polyurethane, neoprene

#### Composites
- **Carbon Fiber**: High strength, lightweight (aerospace)
- **Fiberglass**: Good strength, lower cost (marine, automotive)
- **Aramid (Kevlar)**: Impact resistance (armor, protective gear)

#### Ceramics
- **Alumina**: Wear resistance, insulation
- **Silicon Carbide**: High temperature, abrasion
- **Zirconia**: Biocompatibility, toughness

#### Advanced Materials
- **Aerogels**: Ultra-low density, thermal insulation
- **Graphene**: Extreme strength, conductivity
- **Shape Memory Alloys**: Nitinol (medical devices, actuators)
- **Metamaterials**: Engineered electromagnetic properties

### Testing Standards
- **ASTM**: American Society for Testing and Materials
- **ISO**: International Organization for Standardization
- **ASME**: American Society of Mechanical Engineers
- **SAE**: Society of Automotive Engineers

## 8. Patent Strategy

### Claim Types
1. **Method Claims**: Process steps
2. **System Claims**: Apparatus configuration
3. **Composition Claims**: Material formulations
4. **Use Claims**: Applications of known materials

### Patentability Requirements
- **Novelty**: Not disclosed in prior art
- **Non-Obviousness**: Not obvious to person skilled in art
- **Utility**: Useful application
- **Enablement**: Sufficient detail to reproduce

### Patent Strength Indicators
- **Claim Breadth**: Broader = stronger (if enforceable)
- **Prior Art Distance**: How different from existing tech
- **Commercial Value**: Market size, licensing potential
- **Infringement Detection**: Can you catch infringers?
